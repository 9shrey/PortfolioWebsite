## The debate

The topic of nature versus nurture has been a huge point of contention for psychologists trying to determine the cause of different human behavior. Both theories have proof in their support, but neither is complete. I like to believe in a mixture of both, but let's focus on nurture, since it's the only side we can actually do something about.

## Why peers matter after childhood

Family helps shape you into the person you are until you turn maybe 10 or 12, though its influence never fully goes away. After that, most of the growth and arrival of new ideas is decided by the people you surround yourself with. A lot of people say you are the average of the 5 people you hang out with most often. This is a very agreeable statement, but it's not the entire truth, now is it? Half the time you picked those people because you were already like them. All of us behave differently with different people, and a person can't always be in an "I want to improve as a person" state of mind without causing some serious mental issues (due to isolation or excess stress). Every person needs some whimsy in their life.

## Sorting your friends

For this reason, we separate our friends into multiple categories based on how comfortable we are letting out our childlike, stupid selves in front of them. The people you surround yourself with can be much worse than you at a lot of things and can still push you higher, because they are honest and want the best for you. On the other side of things, if you hang out with snobs who are successful and smart but don't care about you or your success, you may pick up a few skills from them, but they won't pull you up, and I doubt you will be happy at the end of the day.

## Burnout and consistency

Social connection helps prevent burnout, so your performance doesn't dip as much and you can climb at a steady rate. Consistency is more important than bursts of effort, and burnout becomes much more likely if you don't like, or aren't liked by, the people around you.

## Who you should hang out with

So based on what criteria should you hang out with people, you may ask. I think it's fairly simple: hang out with people you like and can learn something from. Anything. People who want success (current state doesn't matter) and want you to succeed. Don't go for the big boats at the dock. Go for the tide that raises all the boats along with it. Better yet, aim to be the tide (not something for everyone). A band of smaller boats going in the right direction is better than a yacht that will leave you lost in the middle of the ocean later.

## Partner, friends and family

Family can help and support you in tough times, but more so as a backup. For my life, I think this is the order of people who shape you day to day: partner, friends, and then family. Unfortunately, this is also the order of who is most likely to betray me, so the order of backups is the reverse. Friends have got you when your partner doesn't, and your family has you when no one does.

Family comes first as a safety net, but partner and friends matter more for who you become. Unlike family, choosing your friends and partner is under your control, so focusing on that is ideal.

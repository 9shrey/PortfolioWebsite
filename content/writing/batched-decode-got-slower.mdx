On a GTX 1650 Mobile, fp16 decode at batch 2 ran at **11.4 tok/s** against **53.9 at batch 1**: batching made it 4.7× worse, and stock PyTorch didn't recover single-sequence throughput until batch 16.

<RooflineFigure caption="cuBLAS falls off the roof at batch 2; the split-K kernel climbs back to 52% of it." />

## The symptom, and why nobody notices it

- Table: stock tok/s by batch (1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32). "Read the column downward."
- Most benchmarks report batch 1 or a big batch; the cliff lives in between.

## Ruling out the cheap explanations

- Bandwidth contention, batching overhead, Python latency: what evidence killed each?
- Nsight: cuBLAS dispatches a tensor-core GEMM at M ≥ 2. TU117 has none.
- Prior art: vllm-sm75-throughput (with a T4 control), pytorch#121957. The diagnosis replicates; the fix is new.

## Four kernels, each chosen by the last one's bottleneck

- v1 `tl.dot` padded to 16: compute wasted on padding (18% of roof)
- v2 explicit reduction: registers → occupancy collapse (29%). Saturated neither compute (49/386 GFLOP/s) nor bandwidth (24/104 GB/s); work that saturates neither is waiting. That chose split-K.
- v3 split-K: still a 3D intermediate (39%)
- v4 split-K + 2D intermediate: registers flat in M (52%)

## The bug that was only wrong when benchmarked

- v3 measured 6.6× faster with relative error 6×10².
- `tl.atomic_add` isn't idempotent; the autotuner re-ran the kernel into the same buffer once per config.

## 8.9× kernel, 5.2× end-to-end

- 94.4% of the baseline step was linear layers; predicted 35.50 ms/tok, measured 35.50.
- An infinitely fast GEMM caps this model at 18×.

## Making the benchmark hard to fool

- Interleaved arms, clock and temperature sampled per measurement.
- Negative controls: batch 32 (0.99×), prefill (0.97–1.08×), batch 1 routed to cuBLAS (1.01×).
